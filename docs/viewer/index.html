What am I looking at?

One building, three copies. Each copy is run by a different dispatch rule — the rule that decides which car answers each request. All three receive exactly the same people at the same moments, so any difference you see is the rule, nothing else.

The question the page answers: which rule makes people wait least? The rules are ranked live by average wait — including the wait so far of anyone still standing — and the verdict appears when everyone has been delivered.

The three rules

Smart dispatchEstimates every passenger's time to destination and picks the car that adds the least total delay for everyone. This is how modern destination-dispatch systems work (ETD).
Nearest carSends the closest car already heading the right way, ignoring how many stops it has queued. Simple, and it degrades under load.
Take turnsCars take requests in strict rotation, wherever they happen to be. Uses no information at all — the baseline.

Reading a building

Amber dots on the left of a floor: people waiting there. More than five shows as +N. Hollow violet dots on the right: people who want to reach that floor, waiting or already riding. A car, coloured by its rule. It fills up as people board; the number is how many are aboard. An amber ring means the doors are open.

The big number is the average wait so far in ticks; beside it, how many are waiting right now, the longest anyone has waited, and how many have waited more than 30 ticks — about a minute, at the two seconds per floor a real car manages. Green marks the best of the three, red the worst. One tick is one floor of travel; the setup line sets what a stop costs (one tick by default; “stops free” is the brief's literal model).

Car usage, under each building, shows one bar per car: how much of the run that car has been busy — carrying someone, or on its way to someone assigned to it. The caption gives the average across cars (how much car-time the rule spends) and the gap between the busiest and least busy car (how evenly it spreads the work). A rule can win on waiting time while working one car much harder than the others; take turns is even by construction but spends the most car-time. The Details view has the per-car numbers.

Trying things

Set the traffic and the building at the top, then Apply & restart. While it runs, add people from the right or click a floor to place someone with a destination. “Keep traffic flowing” keeps people arriving. Adding people after everyone is delivered resumes the run. Space plays and pauses, ← → step one tick.
