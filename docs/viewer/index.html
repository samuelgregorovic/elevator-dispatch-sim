How it works

Traffic. Pick a situation — a morning rush, lunch hour, a 51-floor tower — or upload your own CSV in the brief's format (time,id,source,dest). All three rules always receive exactly the same passengers and run in step, so switching the view never changes the clock.

Rules. Smart dispatch estimates every passenger's time to destination and picks the car that adds the least total delay — the method behind modern destination-dispatch systems (ETD). Nearest car sends the closest car heading the right way, ignoring how many stops it already has. Take turns rotates through the cars regardless of where they are.

Reading the building. Amber dots on the left are people waiting on that floor; hollow dots on the right are people who want to reach that floor (waiting or already riding). A car's outline turns amber while its doors are open. The three numbers are average wait so far, people waiting right now, and the longest anyone has waited; green is the best of the three rules, red the worst. Standings rank the rules by average wait, live.

One tick is one floor of travel; each stop costs one tick. The building is set before a run (Apply & restart). The engine is a JavaScript port of the Python simulation, verified identical on every committed scenario by an automated test. Keyboard: space play/pause, ← → step. Design notes · full results.
